```timeline teal
# Stone Age – सभ्यता की नींव

70000 BCE | Indian subcontinent में **Homo sapiens** (आधुनिक मानव) का पहला appearance। [green]  
यहीं से history की शुरुआत होती है। इस समय मानव **hunter-gatherer** था, पूरी तरह nature पर निर्भर। Nomadic life, caves और पेड़ों की branches को shelter बनाता था।

::callout
type: info
title: 🔍 Lithic Age – "Stone" क्यों?
इस period को **Stone Age** कहते हैं क्योंकि यहाँ से मिले **tools** और **implements** primarily stone के बने हैं – हाँ, bone और wood के भी मिलते हैं, लेकिन mainly stone। Tool making technique में धीरे-धीरे advancement हुआ – crude core से लेकर sharp blades और microliths तक।
::

60000 BCE | Lower Paleolithic – **Core tools** और crude implements। Early humans ने **hammer stones** का use करके **flakes** निकालना शुरू किया। [emerald]  
Important sites: **Sohan Valley** (Pakistan Punjab), **Didwana** (Rajasthan), **Bhimbetka** (Madhya Pradesh), **Patan** (Maharashtra, Tapi river), **Nevasa** (Godavari), **Attirampakkam** (Tamil Nadu).

::cards
### Lower Paleolithic Features (teal)
- Tools: **Core stones** – blunt, crude
- Technique: Direct percussion – striking two stones
- Life: Strictly hunter-gatherer, nomadic
- Shelter: Caves, rock overhangs
---
### Middle to Upper Paleolithic (orange)
- Tools: **Flake tools** – sharper, more effective
- New material: **Flint** (easier to shape)
- Innovations: **Blades** and **burins** (gradual cutting tools)
- Sites: Same regions, continuous occupation
::

50000 BCE | Middle Paleolithic – **Flake tools** common; early humans start using **flint** for sharper implements. [amber]

30000 BCE | Upper Paleolithic – Refined **blades** and **burins**; greater hunting efficiency.

::highlight
color: cyan
icon: 🪨
title: Technological Progression
जैसे-जैसे human needs बदलीं, नए tool types और techniques emerged। **Core → Flake → Blade** की ये trajectory continuous innovation दिखाती है – climate बदला, तो नए tools बने।
::

12000 BCE | Mesolithic (Middle Stone Age) – Climate change: last Ice Age (Pleistocene) से Holocene (present epoch) में transition। Warmer, humid conditions ने vegetation और animal life को बदल दिया। [rose]  
Smaller, faster animals dominated। Early humans ने **microliths** – tiny geometric tools (triangular, crescent-shaped) – develop किए, जिन्हें handles पर haft किया जाता था। **Bow and arrow** appear। Fishing और **domestication of animals** शुरू।

::stats
70,000 | Years of Paleolithic occupation | – | amber
10,000 | Years of Mesolithic transition | – | teal
5 mm | Typical microlith size | – | green
::

::quote
Necessity is the mother of invention – जैसे climate shifted, humans ने smaller, more efficient tools से adapt किया।
by: Archaeological Consensus
::

::grid
Climate Shift | Warming and humidification after the last Ice Age.
---
Fauna Change | Smaller, faster animals; grassland species proliferate.
---
Human Response | New tools (microliths), fishing, and animal domestication.
---
Key Mesolithic Sites
**Adamgarh** (MP) and **Bagor** (Bhilwara, Rajasthan) – earliest evidence of animal domestication in the subcontinent.
::

8000 BCE | Neolithic (New Stone Age) – **Neolithic Revolution**: agriculture starts on a stable basis। Humans **producers** बन गए, सिर्फ gatherers नहीं। [emerald]  
**Settled life** emerges। Villages appear। **Weaving, pottery, and polished stone tools** – ये सब इस transformative period को mark करते हैं।

::callout
type: success
title: 🌾 Neolithic "Revolutionary" क्यों है?
Hunter-gathering से settled agriculture की shift **irreversible** थी और इसने human society को fundamentally change कर दिया:
- Permanent settlements
- Surplus food production
- Crafts (pottery, weaving) का emergence
- Population growth
- सभी later civilisations की foundation
::

7000 BCE | **Mehrgarh** (Balochistan, Pakistan) – Indian subcontinent में **wheat, rice और cotton cultivation** का earliest evidence। [green]  
Other Neolithic sites: **Burzahom** (Jammu & Kashmir – pit dwellings), **Gufkral** (J&K), **Mahagara-Koldihwa** (UP – rice), **Paiyampalli** (South), **Daojali Hading** (Assam – North East).

::stats
9000 | Years ago – earliest Mehrgarh settlement | – | green
3 | Crops cultivated (wheat, rice, cotton) | – | amber
40+ | Neolithic sites identified in India | – | blue
::

::keyval
# Neolithic Tools & Innovations
Polished stone axes (celts) | For clearing forests and agriculture
Ring stones | Weights or mace-heads
Burnished ware | Fine pottery with smooth surface
Pit dwellings | Burzahom – dug into ground for insulation
::

label: 🔄 Transition
title: Neolithic से Chalcolithic की ओर
```

```timeline cyan
# Chalcolithic – धातु युग की शुरुआत

4000 BCE | **Chalcolithic Age** starts – इसका मतलब **Copper + Stone** (Chalco = copper, Lithic = stone)। Early humans ने stone के साथ-साथ **copper** tools और implements बनाना शुरू किया। [orange]

::callout
type: info
title: 🔶 Chalcolithic – क्या है खास?
- **First metal** used – copper
- Rural communities – **well-planned** settlements (unlike haphazard Neolithic villages)
- Agriculture – **crop rotation** and **irrigation** evidence (Inamgaon)
- Crafts – diversified but **limited specialization**
- Trade – **barter** system, limited
- **Urban life** absent – no cities, no writing
::

::highlight
color: amber
icon: 🌾
title: Agricultural Advancements
Neolithic से compare करें तो agriculture में expansion हुआ। **Crop rotation** और **irrigation** के evidence मिले हैं – खासकर **Inamgaon** (Maharashtra, Ghod river) से, जहाँ **granaries** भी मिली हैं। **Navdatoli** (Narmada) से diverse crops के evidence मिले हैं।
::

::cards
### Major Chalcolithic Cultures (teal)
**Ahar-Banas Culture** (Rajasthan) – Ahar, Gilund, Balathal – yellow-red soil, burnt bricks.
---
**Kayatha Culture** (Chambal valley) – Kayatha, Eran – earliest chalcolithic in central India.
---
**Malwa Culture** (Malwa plateau) – Nagda, Navdatoli – widespread, well-planned settlements.
---
**Jorwe Culture** (Maharashtra) – Daimabad, Inamgaon – Godavari-Pravara basin, **proto-urban** sites.
---
**Ganeshwar-Jodhpura Culture** (Rajasthan) – sikar, Jhunjhunu – exploited **Khetri copper mines**.
::

2500 BCE | Jorwe Culture peaks at **Daimabad** – bronze casting evidence, but still rural. [rose]

::stats
4000 | BCE – Chalcolithic begins | – | cyan
1000 | BCE – End of Chalcolithic (Iron Age begins) | – | amber
15+ | Major Chalcolithic cultures identified | – | green
::

::note
Inamgaon | Crop rotation, irrigation, and granary evidence – shows agricultural advancement.
Navdatoli | Diverse crops – wheat, barley, lentil, pea – cultivation evidence.
Balathal | Burnt brick houses – rare in Chalcolithic.
::

::quote
The Chalcolithic communities were rural, but their settlements were far more planned than their Neolithic predecessors.
by: Prof. V. K. Thakur
::

1000 BCE | Chalcolithic Age ends – **Iron Age** begins in Indian subcontinent। [green]
```

```section
title: Indus Valley Civilisation – सिंधु-सरस्वती सभ्यता (c. 2600 – 1900 BCE)
```

```timeline indigo
# Indus Valley – पहली शहरी सभ्यता

2600 BCE | **Mature Harappan Phase** starts – Indian subcontinent की **first urban civilisation**। [purple]  
Core area: **Indus-Saraswati river valleys** (Punjab, Sindh, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Haryana, UP). This is a **Bronze Age** culture – they knew copper + tin = bronze.

::callout
type: success
title: 🏙️ Harappan Civilisation – क्यों खास?
- **First urban civilisation** of India
- **Bronze Age** culture (copper + tin alloy)
- **Planned cities** – grid pattern, drainage, citadel
- **Writing** – undeciphered script (400+ symbols)
- **Standardised weights & measures** – binary progression
- **Long-distance trade** – Mesopotamia, Oman, Afghanistan
- **Surplus agriculture** – wheat, barley, cotton, rice ( Lothal )
::

::highlight
color: cyan
icon: 🏺
title: Pottery & Craft Specialisation
Harappan pottery – **red ware** and **black-on-red ware**। Potter's wheel का use mass production के लिए। Terracotta figurines, bronze casting (Dancing Girl), steatite seals – ये सब high craft specialisation दिखाते हैं।
::

Important Sites:

2600 BCE | **Mohenjo-Daro** (Sindh, Indus river) – "Mound of the Dead"। Great Bath, granary, assembly hall, and **priest-king** sculpture। [emerald]

2600 BCE | **Harappa** (Punjab, Ravi river) – First excavated site (D.R. Sahani, 1921)। **City walls, grid layout, and cemeteries**। [emerald]

2500 BCE | **Lothal** (Gujarat, Gulf of Cambay) – **Dockyard**, bead factory, rice cultivation, and **terracotta models of ships**। [orange]

2500 BCE | **Kalibangan** (Rajasthan, Ghaggar-Hakra) – **Ploughed field** – earliest evidence of agriculture। **Fire altars** – ritual practices। [orange]

2400 BCE | **Dholavira** (Gujarat, Kutch) – **Water reservoir system** – series of dams, underground channels। **Large stone inscriptions** – one of the longest Harappan texts। [rose]

2400 BCE | **Rakhigarhi** (Haryana) – **Largest Harappan site** in Indian subcontinent। [rose]

::cards
### Urban Planning Features (teal)
- **Grid pattern** – roads cutting at 90°
- **Covered drains** – with manholes for cleaning
- **Citadel** (upper town) – for elites, public buildings
- **Lower town** – for commoners, residential areas
---
### Great Bath (Mohenjo-Daro) (blue)
- **Large brick-lined pool**
- Ritual bathing – religious importance
- Steps, changing rooms, and water-tight seal
- One of the earliest public water tanks
---
### Trade & Economy (orange)
- **Weights:** Cubical chert weights (binary: 1,2,4,8,16,32,64)
- **Seals:** Steatite, with animal motifs (unicorn, bull, tiger)
- **Exports:** Cotton textiles, ivory, beads, lapis lazuli, carnelian
- **Imports:** Copper, tin, gold, silver, semi-precious stones
::

::grid
Geographical Extent (Mature Phase)
North: **Manda** (Jammu)  
East: **Alamgirpur** (UP)  
South: **Daimabad** (Maharashtra, Godavari)  
West: **Sutkagen-Dor** (Makran coast, Pakistan)
---
Decline Factors (c. 1900 BCE)
Primary: **Ecological degradation** – deforestation → reduced rainfall → desertification.
Other factors: Tectonic activity changing river courses, flood, and possibly Aryan migration.
::

::keyval
# Harappan Script & Symbols
Writing | Undeciphered – 400+ distinct symbols
Direction | Mostly right-to-left (occasionally boustrophedon)
Medium | Steatite seals, copper plates, pottery
Symbols | Unicorn, pipal tree, swastika, cross, grid
::

1900 BCE | **Decline of Indus Valley** – urban life disappears। **Writing, planned cities, long-distance trade, and elite culture** gradually fade। Rural communities practicing subsistence agriculture re-emerge। [maroon]

::quote
Forests precede civilisations, deserts follow them.
by: Ancient Indian Ecological Wisdom
::

::callout
type: danger
title: ⚠️ Decline – Key Reasons
- **Ecological degradation** – deforestation reduced rainfall, turned the region into desert.
- **River course changes** – tectonic activity shifted river routes.
- **Climate change** – reduced monsoon, less flooding, agricultural collapse.
- **Loss of surplus** – crafts, trade, and urban life become unsustainable.
- After decline, India re-enters a rural, agrarian phase.
::
```

```timeline green
# Vedic Age – आर्यों का आगमन और नई संस्कृति

1500 BCE | **Early Vedic Period** starts। Aryans settle in the **Saptasindhu region** – Indus and its tributaries (Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) + Saraswati river। [green]

::callout
type: info
title: 📖 हमें इस period के बारे में कैसे पता चला?
Primary source: **Rigveda** – the oldest of the four Vedas (Rig, Sama, Yajur, Atharva)। Rigveda describes early Aryans as **tribal, pastoral, semi-nomadic** communities। They were **horse-riding** (horse evidence) and used **spoked wheels** (unlike Harappan solid wheels)।
::

::highlight
color: orange
icon: 🐎
title: Early Vedic – Key Features
- **Pastoral economy** – cattle rearing (wealth measured in cows)
- **Limited agriculture** – only one crop mentioned: **barley (yava)**
- **Copper tools** – Iron not yet discovered
- **Tribal polity** – chief (rajan) was one among equals; no standing army
- **Voluntary taxes** – called **bali** (offerings)
- **Simple religion** – nature worship, yajnas, **no cattle sacrifice**
- **Material well-being** was the focus – not moksha or liberation
::

::cards
### Early Vedic Society (teal)
- Tribal, **egalitarian**
- No fixed social hierarchy
- Women could compose hymns (e.g., Lopamudra, Ghosha)
---
### Early Vedic Economy (orange)
- Cattle as currency
- Barter trade – limited
- No coinage
- Temporary settlements called **grama**
---
### Early Vedic Religion (purple)
- Nature gods: Indra, Agni, Varuna, Soma
- Rituals: Yajnas, offerings of ghee & grains
- No image worship – no temples
- No complex rituals – simple, household-level
::

1000 BCE | **Iron Age** begins in Indian subcontinent। **Iron tools** (ploughshare, axe) का उपयोग शुरू होता है। [rose]

::callout
type: success
title: ⚒️ Iron – गेम चेंजर
Iron tools ने **dense vegetation** – especially in the **upper Gangetic plains** (western UP, Haryana, Delhi) – को clear किया। **Iron ploughshare** से agriculture expand हुआ। **Iron axes** से forest clearance संभव हुआ। इसी से **Later Vedic period** और **state formation** की शुरुआत हुई।
::

1000 BCE | **Later Vedic Period** starts। Expansion into the **Ganga-Yamuna doab**। Important sites: **Hastinapur** (UP), **Kaushambi** (UP)। [emerald]

::highlight
color: teal
icon: 🌾
title: Later Vedic – Transformations
- Agriculture becomes the **mainstay** of the economy
- Permanent settlements (grama, nagara)
- **State formation** begins – rajan's position becomes hereditary
- **Taxes** become compulsory (balī – now mandatory)
- **Varna system** – Brahmin → Kshatriya → Vaishya → Shudra hierarchy solidifies
- **Ritualism** – cattle sacrifices, complex rituals – priestly monopoly
- **Social discrimination** – women lose Vedic rights (can't study, perform yajnas); Shudras excluded
- **Reaction** – rise of heterodox sects (Jainism, Buddhism, Upanishads) in the post-Vedic period
::

::note
Gotra exogamy | Marriage within same gotra prohibited – emerged in this period.
Upanishads | Core meaning of Vedas – "sacrifice of ego, not cattle" – against ritualism.
Cattle sacrifice | Excessive ritualism – led to economic strain and social reaction.
::

600 BCE | End of Vedic period – **Post-Vedic phase** begins। **Mahajanapadas** emerge। [orange]

::quote
The Later Vedic period laid the foundation for state formation, urbanization, and the rise of Magadha – the first historical empires.
by: Prof. R.S. Sharma
::
```

```timeline orange
# Post-Vedic – Second Urbanisation और State Formation

600 BCE | **Second Urbanisation** begins – in the middle and lower Gangetic plains (Bihar, UP, Bengal)। [green]

- **Fertile alluvial soil** – Ganga plain was highly productive
- **Rivers** – easy transportation, communication, army mobilisation
- **Surplus agriculture** – supported crafts, trade, and population growth
- **Population growth** – more demand for goods and services
- **Craft specialisation** – dedicated artisans, weavers, potters, metalworkers
- **Trade expansion** – both internal and external (silk route)
- **State revenue** – surplus agriculture → regular taxes → stronger states
::

Brahmanical ritualism और cattle sacrifices से people fed up हो गए थे। Varna system की rigidity, priestly monopoly, और economic burden (cattle sacrifice) – इन सबके खिलाफ **heterodox movements** emerged। Jainism और Buddhism सबसे popular बने। इस period को **Age of Buddha** भी कहते हैं।
::

::keyval
# Mahajanapada Capitals & Highlights

600 BCE | **Rise of Magadha** – factors behind its power: [emerald]  
1. **Fertile soil** – Gangetic plains  
2. **Good rainfall** – high agricultural productivity  
3. **Iron mines** – Jharkhand – superior tools & weapons  
4. **Strategic capitals** – Rajgir (hill-fortified) → Pataliputra (surrounded by rivers – Ganga, Son, Punpun – natural defence)  
5. **Rivers for communication** – easy mobilisation and trade  
6. **Strong rulers** – Bimbisara, Ajatashatru, Shishunaga, Kalashoka, Mahapadma Nanda, Chandragupta Maurya

::quote
Magadha’s rise was not just due to geography – its rulers were visionary and ambitious.
by: Historical consensus
```

```timeline purple
# Mauryan Empire – India का पहला साम्राज्य

321 BCE | **Chandragupta Maurya** establishes the Mauryan Empire – first **all-India empire**। [gold]  
**Chanakya** (Kautilya/Vishnugupta) – chief architect – wrote **Arthashastra** (statecraft, economics, military strategy)।

::callout
type: info
title: 📜 Arthashastra – प्राचीन राजनीति का ग्रंथ
Kautilya ने **Arthashastra** – राजनीति, अर्थशास्त्र, और सैन्य रणनीति – पर एक comprehensive treatise लिखा। इसमें राजा के duties, taxation, espionage, diplomacy, और war ethics का detail में वर्णन है।
::

305 BCE | Chandragupta defeats Seleucus Nicator (Greek general) – gains **territories in Afghanistan, Balochistan**। [emerald]

::stats
321 | BCE – Mauryan Empire established | – | purple
305 | BCE – Seleucus defeated | – | green
40,000 | Soldiers – Mauryan army (approx.) | – | amber
::

::cards
### Mauryan Administration (teal)
- **Centralised bureaucracy** – extensive hierarchy
- **Provincial governors** (Kumara) – for key provinces
- **Spies** – detailed intelligence network
- **Taxation** – land revenue (1/6 of produce)
- **Public works** – irrigation, roads, wells
---
### Sources of Information (orange)
- **Arthashastra** – Kautilya
- **Indica** – Megasthenes (Greek ambassador to Chandragupta)
- **Ashokan inscriptions** – major & minor rock edicts, pillar edicts
- **Buddhist texts** – Mahavamsa, Dipavamsa
---
### Military & Economy (purple)
- Large standing army
- Royal monopoly on mines, forests, and irrigation
- State-owned factories
- Trade guilds regulated
- Coinage – punch-marked coins
::

268 BCE | **Ashoka** ascends the throne – grandsion of Chandragupta। [rose]

261 BCE | **Kalinga War** – Ashoka defeated Kalinga (coastal Odisha) – **massive bloodshed** – changed Ashoka's life। [maroon]

::callout
type: danger
title: ☸️ Kalinga War – धम्म की जीत
Kalinga में करीब 1,00,000 लोग मारे गए और 1,50,000 को captive बनाया गया। This bloodshed **transformed Ashoka** – he embraced **Buddhism** and started **Dhamma** – a policy of non-violence, religious tolerance, social harmony, and welfare.
::

::highlight
color: cyan
icon: ☸️
title: Ashoka's Dhamma – Universal Ethical Code
- **Religious tolerance** – respect for all sects
- **Social harmony** – no discrimination
- **Non-violence** – opposed animal sacrifice, hunting
- **Welfare measures** – hospitals, wells, rest houses
- **Paternal kingship** – king as father of the people
- **Missionaries** – sent to Sri Lanka, West Asia, Greece
- **Inscriptions** – written in Prakrit (Brahmi and Kharosthi scripts) – language of the common people
::

::grid
Major Edicts – Key Messages
1. Prohibition of animal slaughter
2. Welfare measures (hospitals, roads)
3. Respect for Brahmins and ascetics
4. Court procedures – fair trials
5. International relations – peaceful coexistence
---
Pillar Edicts – Locations
- Lauriya-Nandangarh (Bihar)
- Lauriya-Araraj (Bihar)
- Rampurva (Bihar)
- Sankisya (UP)
- Sarnath (UP – lion capital – national emblem)
- Sanchi (MP)
::

::note
Lion Capital – Sarnath | 4 lions back-to-back – national emblem of India.
Bairat | Temple of Buddha – earliest Buddhist structural temple.
Dhauli | Kalinga edict – mentions **"save lives of humans and animals"**.
::

232 BCE | Ashoka dies – empire begins to decline। [maroon]

185 BCE | **Last Mauryan ruler – Brihadratha** – assassinated by his Brahmin commander **Pushyamitra Shunga** – Shunga dynasty established in the east। [maroon]

::quote
The Mauryan Empire – India’s first experiment with imperial unity – collapsed due to weak successors, financial crisis, over-extension, and Brahminical backlash against Ashoka’s policies.
by: Historical Analysis
::

::callout
type: warning
title: ⚔️ Mauryan Decline – कारण
- **Weak successors** after Ashoka
- **Financial crisis** – liberal grants by Ashoka
- **Over-extension** – vast territory difficult to control
- **Brahminical opposition** – resentment against Ashoka's pro-Buddhist policies
- **Provincial governors** – carving out independent territories
- **External invasions** – from the north-west (Indo-Greeks, Sakas, Parthians, Kushanas)
- **End of central authority** – disintegration into eastern and western parts
::
```

```timeline pink
Kushanas controlled the **Silk Route** (China ↔ Central Asia ↔ India ↔ Mediterranean) – they taxed trade and became immensely wealthy। **Kanishka** – greatest Kushana ruler – ruled from **Purushapura (Peshawar)** and **Mathura** (second capital)।

::highlight

- **Coins** – gold, silver, copper – with Greek and Brahmi legends

- **Fourth Buddhist Council** – Kundalvana (Kashmir) – Buddhism split into Mahayana & Hinayana
- **Trade** – extensive – textiles, spices, gems exported
- **Cultural synthesis** – Indian, Greek, Persian, Central Asian

- Ruled: NW India, Punjab, Sindh, Gujarat, Malwa, UP, Bihar?

These communities were primarily **pastoral**, with limited agriculture। They used **iron tools** – mostly for hunting। Burials were covered with **huge stones** (megaliths) – urns containing skeletons, iron objects, and pottery – found in plateaus and upland areas.
```

```timeline gold
# Gupta Empire – Classical Age का स्वर्णिम दौर

319 CE | **Chandragupta I** – first independent Gupta ruler – takes title **Maharajadhiraja** – starts Gupta era। [gold]

::callout
type: success
title: ⚔️ Samudragupta – दिग्विजयी सम्राट
His **Allahabad Pillar Inscription** (composed by court poet Harisena) – describes his conquests:  
- **Northern India** – defeated Naga, Western kingdoms  
- **Eastern India** – crossed Ganga, defeated 12 rulers in coastal Odisha, Bengal  
- **Deep South** – reached Kanchipuram (Pallava capital) – defeated Vishnugopa – **liberated** him (accepted supremacy, paid tribute)  
- Took **Ashvamedha** – performed horse sacrifice – issued coins
::

::highlight

icon: 🏅
title: Gupta Golden Age – क्यों Golden था?
- **Literature** – Kalidasa (Shakuntala, Meghaduta), Vishakhadatta (Mudrarakshasa), Amarasimha (Amarakosha) – Sanskrit renaissance  
- **Science** – Aryabhata (mathematics, astronomy), Varahamihira (astrology), Sushruta (surgery), Charaka (medicine)  
- **Art & Architecture** – Dashavatara Temple (Deogarh), iron pillar of Delhi (rust-free) – **classical sculpture** – Buddha images at Sarnath & Mathura  
- **Administration** – decentralised – feudal chiefs, land grants to Brahmins and officials – but efficient revenue collection  
- **Economy** – thriving trade – but decline in silk trade with Byzantium later  
- **Education** – Nalanda University (established by Kumaragupta I – c. 450 CE) – international students

::cards
### Gupta Administration (teal)
- **Feudal structure** – feudatory chiefs (tributaries)
- **Land grants** – to Brahmins, officials (replacing cash salaries)
- **Revenue** – land tax (1/6), trade taxes
- **Military** – no large standing army – feudatories provided troops
---
### Gupta Decline – Factors (maroon)
- **Huna invasions** (450-512 CE) – Toramana, Mihirakula – destroyed trade, law & order
- **Loss of Silk Route** – Byzantines learned silk making – trade declined
- **Succession conflicts** – weak rulers, primogeniture not well-established
- **Financial crisis** – land grants reduced state revenue
- **Officials** – multiple roles (Harisena – poet + chief justice + minister) – concentration of power
- **Feudatories rebelled** – stopped paying tribute, carved out independent territories
- **Internal law & order** – broke down – internal trade affected
- **Yashodharman** (Malwa feudatory) – defeated Huns in 512 CE – asserted independence
::

::quote
The Gupta Empire – a golden age of art, literature, and science – declined due to economic collapse triggered by Huna invasions and the breakdown of long-distance trade.
by: Prof. A.L. Basham
::

::note
Nalanda University | Kumaragupta I – 450 CE – world's first residential university – declined after Huna invasions and revived later.
Iron Pillar | Mehrauli, Delhi – Chandragupta II – rust-free iron – metallurgical marvel.

::callout

title: 📜 Harsha – The Last Great Hindu Emperor
- Ruled 606-647 CE – almost 40 years

- **Ambition** – wanted to expand southwards – but was **defeated by Pulakeshin II** (Chalukya of Badami) on the banks of Narmada
```
