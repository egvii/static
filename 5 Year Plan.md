# India's Five Year Plans – Complete Chronology

1st Plan | 1951 – 1956 [rose]
  Model | **Harrod-Domar Model**  
  Focus | **Agriculture** 🌾 – priority to food security and rural development.
  Irrigation projects, community development programs, and land reforms initiated.  
  Target Growth: 2.1% | Actual Growth: **3.6%** ✅

::cards
### Key Projects – 1st Plan (teal)
- **Bhakra Nangal Dam** – multipurpose river valley project
- **Hirakud Dam** – Odisha
- **Damodar Valley Corporation** – flood control & power
---
### Focus Areas (orange)
- Agriculture & irrigation
- Community development
- Land reforms
- Transport & communication
::

2nd Plan | 1956 – 1961 [blue]
  Model | **P.C. Mahalanobis Model** + Feldmann  
  Focus | **Rapid Industrialization** – heavy industries and capital goods.
  **Socialist pattern of society** adopted – public sector dominance.  
  Target Growth: 4.5% | Actual Growth: **4.1%**

::callout
type: success
title: 🏭 2nd Plan – Industrial Revolution
1. **Bhilai Steel Plant** (Chhattisgarh) – with USSR collaboration
2. **Rourkela Steel Plant** (Odisha) – with Germany collaboration
3. **Durgapur Steel Plant** (West Bengal) – with UK collaboration
- **Heavy Engineering Corporation** (HEC) – Ranchi
- **Hindustan Shipyard** – Vishakhapatnam
::

3rd Plan | 1961 – 1966
  Focus | **Self-reliance** – reducing dependence on foreign aid.  
  Agriculture and industry both targeted.  
  Key Events | **Indo-China War (1962)** and **Indo-Pak War (1965)** – impacted economy.  
  **Green Revolution** initiatives started (but major impact came later).  
  Target Growth: 5.6% | Actual Growth: **2.8%** ❌ (due to wars and droughts)

::callout
type: danger
title: ⚠️ 3rd Plan – Challenges
- Wars (China 1962, Pakistan 1965) – diverted resources to defense
- Severe drought (1965-66) – agricultural crisis
- Food shortage – reliance on PL-480 imports (USA)
- Economic instability – led to **Plan Holiday** (1966-1969)
::

Plan Holiday | 1966 – 1969 [green]
  No Five Year Plan – due to economic crisis, wars, and drought.  
  **Annual Plans** were implemented instead (1966-67, 1967-68, 1968-69).  
  **Green Revolution** (1966-67) – introduction of High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds, fertilizers, and irrigation – transformed agriculture.  
  Key Figure | **Dr. M.S. Swaminathan** – father of Green Revolution in India.
  Gihgyuh gyy
  **Devaluation of Rupee** (1966) – to boost exports.

::highlight
color: green
icon: 🌾
title: Green Revolution – 1966-67
- HYV seeds – wheat (Mexico) and rice (IR8 – Philippines)
- Increased use of fertilizers and pesticides
- **Punjab, Haryana, UP** – became the "Granary of India"
- Food production skyrocketed – India became self-sufficient in food grains
- Dr. Norman Borlaug (Nobel Prize) – developed HYV wheat
- Dr. M.S. Swaminathan – adapted it to Indian conditions
::

4th Plan | 1969 – 1974
  Focus | **Growth with Stability** – and **social justice** (Garibi Hatao).  
  Key Events | **Bangladesh Liberation War (1971)** – economic burden.  
  **Bank Nationalization** (1969) – 14 major banks nationalized.  
  **Green Revolution** gains consolidated.  
  Target Growth: 5.7% | Actual Growth: **3.3%** ❌

::cards
### Major Achievements – 4th Plan (teal)
- Green Revolution success – food self-sufficiency
- Bank nationalization – social control over banking
- Monopolies and Restrictive Trade Practices (MRTP) Act – 1969
---
### Challenges (orange)
- Bangladesh war (1971) – refugee crisis & defense spending
- Oil crisis (1973) – global inflation
- Rising prices – food and fuel
::

5th Plan | 1974 – 1979
  Focus : **Garibi Hatao** (Remove Poverty) – al unemployment and poverty.  
  Key Event | **Emergency (1975-1977)** – 20-point program launched.  
  **Nationalization of coal, oil, and general insurance** – under Indira Gandhi.  
  Plan terminated early (1979) – after change of government (Janata Party).  
  Target Growth: 4.4% | Actual Growth: **4.8%** ✅ (until 1978)

::callout
type: info
title: 📜 5th Plan – Key Initiatives
- **20-Point Program** – poverty alleviation, land reforms, minimum wages, and social welfare
- Nationalization of coal mines, oil industry, and general insurance
- **Integrated Rural Development Program (IRDP)** – started
- Focus on employment generation
::


Rolling Plan | 1979 – 1980
  No Five Year Plan – **interim Rolling Plans** – due to political instability.  
  Two Annual Plans (1979-80 and 1980-81) – continued the 5th Plan's framework.  

6th Plan | 1980 – 1985
  Focus | **Direct Anti-Poverty Programs** – and employment generation.  
  Key Programs | **NREP** (National Rural Employment Program), **RLEGP** (Rural Landless Employment Guarantee Program).  
  **Economic liberalization** began – gradual opening of the economy.  
  Target Growth: 5.2% | Actual Growth: **5.4%** ✅

::highlight
color: cyan
icon: 💼
title: 6th Plan – Poverty Alleviation
- **NREP** – National Rural Employment Program – provided jobs in rural areas
- **RLEGP** – Rural Landless Employment Guarantee Program – targeted landless laborers
- Focus on infrastructure, energy, and transport
- India hosted the **1982 Asian Games** – showcase of development
::

7th Plan | 1985 – 1990
  Focus | **Food, Work, and Productivity** – with emphasis on **modernization**.  
  Key Programs | **Jawahar Rozgar Yojana (JRY)** – integrated employment program.  
  **Technology missions** – in areas like immunization, drinking water, and literacy.  
  Target Growth: 5.0% | Actual Growth: **6.0%** ✅ (exceeded target)

::cards
### 7th Plan – Achievements (teal)
- Jawahar Rozgar Yojana – employment for rural poor
- **Technology missions** – immunization (Universal Immunization Program)
- **Operation Blackboard** – primary education improvement
- **Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana (PMGSY)** – rural roads (conceptualized)
---
### Challenges (orange)
- Foreign exchange crisis (1991) – severe balance of payments issue
- Gulf War (1990) – oil prices spiked
- Political instability – mid-term elections in 1989
::

Annual Plans | 1990 – 1992
  No Five Year Plan – due to political instability (V.P. Singh, Chandra Shekhar, Narasimha Rao governments).  
  **Balance of Payments (BOP) crisis (1991)** – India faced foreign exchange crisis.  
  **Economic Reforms (1991)** – LPG (Liberalization, Privatization, Globalization) – launched by Manmohan Singh (Finance Minister) under P.V. Narasimha Rao.  
  **Planning Commission** continued but liberalization reduced its role.

::callout
type: danger
title: 💰 1991 Reforms – The Turning Point
- India faced **foreign exchange crisis** – reserves dropped to $1 billion (just 2 weeks of imports)
- India approached **IMF** for bailout – reforms were conditions
- **LPG Reforms** – Liberalization (reduced regulations), Privatization (disinvestment of PSUs), Globalization (opening to FDI)
- **Manmohan Singh** (FM) and **P.V. Narasimha Rao** (PM) – architects of reform
- **Rupee devalued** – boosted exports
- **FDI allowed** – in many sectors
- **License Raj** abolished – industrial licensing removed (except for strategic sectors)
::

8th Plan | 1992 – 1997
  Focus | **Human Development** – education, health, employment, and poverty reduction.  
  Key Programs | **National Social Assistance Program (NSAP)** – old age pensions, widow pensions.  
  **Economic reforms** continued – accelerated industrial growth.  
  Target Growth: 5.6% | Actual Growth: **6.8%** ✅

::highlight
color: purple
icon: 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦
title: 8th Plan – Human Development Focus
- **National Social Assistance Program (NSAP)** – social security for old age, widows, disabled
- **District Primary Education Program (DPEP)** – universal primary education
- **Mid-Day Meal Scheme** – expanded coverage
- **National Family Health Survey (NFHS)** – first conducted (1992-93)
- **New Economic Policy (NEP)** – continued liberalization, privatization, and globalization
::

9th Plan | 1997 – 2002
  Focus | **Growth with Social Justice and Equity** – inclusive development.  
  Key Programs: **Pradhan Mantri Gramodaya Yojana (PMGY)** – rural development.  
  **Information Technology (IT)** boom – India emerged as a global IT hub.  
  Target Growth: 6.5% | Actual Growth: **5.4%** ❌ (due to global slowdown and Kargil War)

::cards
### 9th Plan – Key Programs (teal)
- **PMGY** – Pradhan Mantri Gramodaya Yojana – rural infrastructure and services
- **Swarnjayanti Gram Swarozgar Yojana (SGSY)** – self-employment for rural poor
- **National Highway Development Project (NHDP)** – Golden Quadrilateral
---
### Challenges (orange)
- Kargil War (1999) – defense spending increased
- Global IT bubble burst (2000) – slowdown
- Government instability – multiple coalition governments
::

10th Plan | 2002 – 2007
  Focus: **Growth with Speed and Social Justice** – aimed at 8% growth.  
  Key Programs: **National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (NREGA)** – later enacted in 2005 (during UPA-1).  
  **Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan (SSA)** – universal elementary education.  
  **Golden Quadrilateral** – completed – highway connectivity.  
  Target Growth: 8.0% | Actual Growth: **7.2%** ✅ (exceeded previous plans)

::callout
type: success
title: 🚀 10th Plan – Rapid Growth
- **NREGA** (later) – 100 days assured employment to rural households
- **Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan** – free and compulsory education for 6-14 years
- **Golden Quadrilateral** – 5,846 km highway connecting Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata
- **National Rural Health Mission (NRHM)** – improved healthcare in rural areas
- **Right to Information Act (2005)** – transparency in governance
- India became the **2nd fastest growing economy** globally (after China)
::

11th Plan | 2007 – 2012
  Focus: **Inclusive Growth** – broader coverage and faster growth.  
  Key Programs: **National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC)** – 8 missions.  
  **Right to Education Act (RTE)** – 2009 – education becomes a fundamental right.  
  **Unique Identification (Aadhaar)** – started in 2010.  
  Target Growth: 9.0% | Actual Growth: **7.9%** ❌ (due to global financial crisis 2008-09)

::highlight
color: orange
icon: 🌍
title: 11th Plan – Climate Action & Rights
- **NAPCC** – 8 national missions on climate change (Solar, Energy Efficiency, Water, etc.)
- **RTE Act (2009)** – Article 21A – free and compulsory education for 6-14 years
- **Aadhaar** – Unique Identification (UID) – began enrolment in 2010
- **National Food Security Act** – 2013 (enacted after the plan)
- **Global Financial Crisis (2008)** – impacted growth but India recovered quickly
- **2G Spectrum Scam** – exposed governance issues
::

12th Plan | 2012 – 2017
  Focus: **Faster, Sustainable, and More Inclusive Growth** – aim for 8% GDP growth.  
  Key Programs: **Make in India** – launched in 2014 – promotion of manufacturing.  
  **Swachh Bharat Abhiyan** – cleanliness drive (2014).  
  **Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY)** – financial inclusion (2014).  
  **Digital India** – technology-enabled governance (2015).  
  **Goods and Services Tax (GST)** – implemented in 2017.  
  **Planning Commission** replaced by **NITI Aayog** (2015) – ending Five Year Plan era.  
  Target Growth: 8.0% | Actual Growth: **6.8%** ❌ (due to global slowdown, implementation challenges)

::callout
type: info
title: 📜 End of Five Year Plans – NITI Aayog (2015)
- **Planning Commission** (established 1950) – replaced by **NITI Aayog** (National Institution for Transforming India) – on 1 January 2015
- NITI Aayog's approach: **"bottom-up"** (state-centric, cooperative federalism) – compared to Planning Commission's **"top-down"** approach
- 12th Five Year Plan (2012-17) – **last Five Year Plan**
- From 2017 onwards – **Three Year Action Agendas** (2017-18 to 2019-20) and **Seven Year Strategy** documents – rolling plans
- **15 Year Vision Document** – 2017-2032 – long-term perspective
::
